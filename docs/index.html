Voice Me
a harmony teacher

Your compositional exploratory tool.

Voice Me is complete with an instructional voicings library, compositional assistant, and grand staff.

Before you start

What you'll need

  • A Mac with Apple Silicon — M1 or later, running macOS 12 or newer.
  • A MIDI keyboard over USB. Plug it in before you launch. No drivers needed for most keyboards.

Installing

Four steps, one of them alarming

  1. Open the DMG and drag Voice Me into Applications.

    The usual window with the arrow. Nothing unusual here.

  2. Clear the quarantine flag in Terminal.

    Open Terminal — press ⌘-Space, type “Terminal”, hit Return — then paste this and hit Return again.

    Terminal 
    xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Voice Me.app"

  3. Open Voice Me normally.

First run

Getting started

Top left you should see a green dot and your keyboard's name. If it says No MIDI device, hit the ↺ button or pick your keyboard from the dropdown.

Then just play. Keys light up as you press them, the chord name appears at the bottom, and the staff fills in. Turn on the Rhodes with the ○ Sound button at the top right — it takes a moment to load the samples the first time.

Compose with me is the interesting part. Press the button at the top, play any chord, and a tree grows with suggestions — each one a named harmonic move with its roman numerals above it. A passing diminished, a secondary dominant, a modal shift.

Reading the keyboard

What the colours are telling you

Click a branch and the on-screen keyboard tells your hands what to do.

Blue
keep holding this one — it's in the next chord too
Gold
press this
Grey
let this one go
Green flash
you got it

Some suggestions are two chords, because the move is the lesson. A7/C♯ → Dm7 is teaching the bass walking C–C♯–D, and the first chord on its own teaches nothing. It'll walk you through both.

Worth trying

  • The Basic / Colorful / Complex dial

    Changes how spicy the suggestions get. Basic sticks to triads and plain sevenths; Complex will offer you altered dominants.

  • Set the key

    This is the one that changes the most. Play a Gm9 on auto and the app has no way to know whether you mean i in G minor or ii in F, so it hedges. Tell it F major and every branch reasons from there — ii → V → I, with passing chords that land inside the key. Some branches modulate; playing one moves the key centre for you.

  • Hover a branch

    You'll get the other colourings of the same chord — F7, F9, F13, F7♭9. Same harmonic job, different clothes.

  • Click any chord in the row under the piano

    It re-cues that voicing so you can get it under your fingers again. This doesn't undo anything — the tree stays where it is.

  • Backspace and Escape

    Backspace removes the last move. Escape stops practising.

Feedback

What I'd love to know

  • Does it name your chords correctly? Especially odd voicings, rootless left-hand shapes, anything with a slash bass.
  • Anything confusing, or any moment you didn't know what to do next.
  • Crashes, stuck notes, keys lighting up wrong.
